# Proposed workflow reference addition: kinetic synthesis and calibration evidence

These lessons arise from the complete main-article and matched-SI review of Peng, Wickham and Alivisatos, JACS 1998, 120, 5343–5344, DOI 10.1021/ja9805425. Apply them when the inspected source supports the distinction.

- **Preserve one-sided numerical bounds.** An injection reported as `<0.1 s` has an exclusive upper bound, not an exact duration of 0.1 s. Use a null scalar value, `maximum: 0.1`, `maximum_exclusive: true`, the original unit and raw wording. Validation, rendered quantities, recipe signatures and training exports must preserve the same meaning. Do not supply an unreported lower bound.
- **Keep sequential additions within their parent experiment.** CdSe reinjection at 190 min and InAs additions at 23 and 158 min are stages of their respective growth trajectories. Preserve elapsed-time origin, dose and source-qualified composition; do not treat each dose or sampled time point as a new synthesis run. Distinguish injection duration from elapsed reaction time.
- **Treat calibration tables according to their provenance.** The SI’s 16 CdSe and 20 InAs rows relate optical peaks to previously TEM-calibrated sizes. Retain every row and its units, including repeated rounded sizes, but do not assign a current recipe, batch or sampling time without evidence. Calibration observations are not independent recipe outcomes or automatic size-conditioned training labels. Verify image content when OCR corrupts table cells.
- **Keep unassigned characterization specimens separate.** The 8.5 nm CdSe TEM image is attributed to distribution focusing but is not linked to the smaller-particle optical trajectory by an exact preparation record. Preserve its reported diameter, scale bar and morphology without filling in growth time, dose sequence, crystal phase or an exact recipe-to-structure label.
- **Attach atmosphere and storage to the correct operation.** Argon is explicit for preparation of the InCl₃–TOP stock; a separate InAs growth-vessel atmosphere is not specified. Cooling and drybox storage belong to that upstream solution, with unreported storage temperature and duration. Do not propagate conditions from an upstream stock, neighboring route or earlier paper into later stages.
- **Preserve technique-specific sampling grids.** The InAs SI absorption and PL panels show different selected times. Store each technique’s labels separately and leave cross-technique specimen identity unresolved where necessary. If the SI omits time units, label the contextual link to the main article rather than presenting the units as printed in the panel. Preserve the restriction to the high-energy PL half when reabsorption affects the other half.
- **Distinguish a reported temperature from a maintained hold.** The CdSe injection is reported to lower temperature to 300 °C. Treat use of 300 °C as a subsequent nominal growth condition as a contextual inference unless the source separately states a maintained hold. Keep InAs 300 °C preheating, 250 °C initial cooling and 260 °C continued growth as distinct stages.
- **Generate material method cards from reviewed records.** A material hub must include every reviewed synthesis route associated with that material, using record type, material association and review eligibility. Do not restrict cards through a hardcoded whitelist of paper prefixes. Keep equal presentation for eligible routes while retaining supporting procedures, observations and calibration records in their appropriate sections. Verify that a newly integrated route appears both in canonical data and in the existing material hub.

The public website, canonical records and task-specific exports should share these distinctions. A successful import or record count alone does not establish that all reviewed routes and source evidence are visible to readers.

## Pressure-cell experiments and comparative evidence

Lessons from Shah et al. (2001), DOI 10.1021/jp011815c; apply when the inspected source supports these distinctions:

- **Represent the pressure-cell topology.** Distinguish reagent injection into the reaction chamber from fluid applied behind a piston to control pressure. Keep initial fluid charge, reaction working volume and injection-loop capacity separate; a loop's capacity is not a delivered dose. Depressurization and venting require their own states, including which material remains for recovery.
- **Separate typical procedures from tabulated variants.** A general mass range does not supply each row's exact mass, and a typical working volume does not justify reconstructing row-specific charges from concentration. Preserve the table's reported means, standard deviations and relative dispersities even when rounded values do not exactly reconcile. An extra plotted point absent from the caption's row list remains unassigned.
- **Prevent shared acquisition records from spreading specimen evidence.** A common TEM/EDS procedure can apply to several materials while a specific EDS figure belongs to only one. Audit material-page filters as well as canonical links: shared procedure IDs must not make an Ag spectrum appear as Ir or Pt evidence. Matching rounded particle diameters across figures and tables is insufficient for a specimen join.
- **Leave ambiguous chemical identity unresolved.** Preserve a repeatedly printed but questionable precursor name and its supplier. Withhold formula, connectivity and 3D geometry until identity is supported; do not silently substitute a chemically familiar analogue. A labeled identity card is preferable to a guessed molecule.
- **Retain model assumptions with model outputs.** Hamaker estimates, assumed optical constants, dimensionless distributions and ideal sticking probabilities need explicit model provenance and variable definitions. Preserve source equation inconsistencies rather than silently repairing them. Particle packing is distinct from atomic crystal symmetry; qualitative crystallinity does not establish a refined phase or make a reused bulk CIF a measured structure label.

For shared reagent specifications, the reader may group identical facts and show their linked-record count. Preserve each canonical source pointer and record link; never use display deduplication to merge independent measurements or outcomes.

## Coating chemistry, analytical states and comparator specimens

Lessons from Gerion et al. (2001), DOI 10.1021/jp0105488; apply when the inspected source supports these distinctions:

- A downstream coating recipe uses a pre-existing core/shell stock. Preserve the upstream citation and stock composition without importing an uninspected core/shell preparation. An optional aminosilane extension with no dose is a partial procedure, not a third complete synthesis route.
- Model analytical outputs as `analysis_data` and collections of separate comparator aliquots as `sample_set`. A paired nanocrystal/dye experiment does not physically mix those samples. Reserve `mixture` for actual material combinations.
- Dialysis contents, centrifugal-membrane retentate, syringe filtrate, precipitated pellet and clarified supernatant are different retained fractions. Apparatus drawings must reflect the source state before the action, and must distinguish membrane concentration from pellet collection.
- A supplier name, formula and printed molecular drawing can conflict. Preserve an unresolved identity card and the original drawing until the identity is established. Elemental-formula agreement alone cannot validate positional isomers; verify substituent connectivity as well.
- A reagent mass stated per unit stock volume is not its total reaction dose. Preserve solute and solvent components separately, stock concentrations or their missingness, optional transfer alternatives and branch inputs.
- Equal color labels, rounded sizes or common characterization methods do not establish a unique recipe–specimen match. Distinguish chromatogram trace colors from emission colors; peak areas from particle-number fractions; AFM heights from single-particle shell thickness; and assay upper estimates from direct surface-group counts.
- Reader measurement labels must use the assigned sample composition or explicitly leave it unassigned. A record-level material formula can describe a broad study context and must not be substituted for the identity of a comparator specimen.
- Preserve conflicts between methods, captions, axes and discussion with their own locators. A longer acquisition stated in prose does not extend the displayed trace; source-stated population exclusions cannot erase conflicting visible bins. Declared but unlocated supporting micrographs remain a named gap.


## Repeated layer exchange and optical acquisition context

Lessons from Braun et al. (2001), DOI10.1021/jp010002l; apply where the inspected source supports them:

- Represent surface replacement differently from deposition. An exchange diagram must conserve its illustrative outer radius, and displaced ions that remain in solution must remain available to a subsequent operation. Repeat-block notation does not authorize extra reagent additions or workup. Conditional precursor additions belong only to their stated branches; missing doses remain unknown.
- Keep an acidification onset distinct from total crystal-growth duration, nominal system core dimensions distinct from conflicting general-preparation sizes, and gas volume distinct from a mole amount without pressure/temperature reference conditions. A generic ion name cannot identify its salt, counterion or hydrate; a stabilizer name need not identify one discrete polymer structure.
- Separate synthesis conditions from analytical room temperature, laser-pump pulse settings from OPO output characterization, delay-stage resolution from instrumental response, pulse energy from fluence, and optical hardware from synthesis ingredients. Preserve the source's own signal-label conflicts.
- A water-reference subtraction that removes Raman background does not supply a Raman spectrum of the material. An architecture drawing or cited wavefunction sketch is not microscopy, measured atomic geometry or an exact-structure training target. Missing intermediate traces and raw matrices remain explicit without invented interpolation or digitization.
- Keep the new material discoverable through the existing periodic table and retain equal method cards on relevant existing component hubs, with whole-heterostructure attribution. Source-level completeness and batch-level training eligibility remain separate judgments.


## Porous hosts, repeated loading and lattice scale

Lessons from Besson et al. (2002), DOI10.1021/nl015685v:

- Preserve host preparation, pore loading and substrate-specific characterization as separate branches. A cadmium-ion-impregnated silica film is not yet a CdS product. Rinsing retains the supported film, and repeated adsorption/precipitation cycles form a trajectory rather than independent synthesis batches. An unnamed copolymer host does not inherit the CTAB recipe.
- Distinguish input-water pH from mixed-sol pH; initial salt concentration from final concentration after additives; molar sol ratios from an unspecified dilution basis; and symbolic atmospheric-pressure endpoints from an assumed numeric pressure. Keep unreported acid, hydrate and polymer identities unresolved.
- Separate mesoscopic pore or nanoparticle-superlattice symmetry from atomic crystal symmetry. An image Fourier power spectrum is not acquired SAED. Explicitly distinguish atomic fringes, low-angle host diffraction, pore-size estimates and optically inferred particle dimensions; do not fabricate an atomic CIF.
- Preserve trace rescaling, caption/text assignment conflicts, method-dependent lattice parameters and differences between model-derived pore filling, qualitative completeness and visible empty pores. Do not silently assign a plotted PL curve to a specimen when its caption conflicts with prose.
- Audit functional-group semantics as well as formula/connectivity: a nitrate ion is not a nitro group. Use host_matrix roles and explicit target-host context in task exports when the source distinguishes templates. Multi-outcome trajectories need explicit outcome selection before size-conditioned training; never let a helper silently choose the first size.


## Melt-derived glass and size-metric provenance

Lessons from Dantas et al. (2002), DOI 10.1021/jp0208743:

- A precursor batch list is not a stoichiometric final-glass formula. Keep powder identities, missing mixture proportions, unspecified dopant source and uncertain introduction timing separate from the host and embedded nanocrystal identity. Do not replace a printed oxide or questionable crucible material with a chemically more familiar alternative without verified evidence.
- Expose shared preparation on each treatment route when this makes the complete process understandable, but mark inherited conditions and common upstream lineage. Several annealing durations and analytical cohorts do not prove separately identified melt batches. Heating, fast cooling, stress relief, sample cutting and polishing require their own states; do not invent a quench medium, polishing solvent or atmosphere.
- Preserve the dimensional meaning of every size: a theoretical radius axis, generic prose size, AFM grain height, lateral feature size and substrate-reference depth are different quantities. Similar values from different cohorts are comparisons, not evidence of physical specimen identity. Ambiguous size metrics must not become diameter-conditioned training labels.
- Keep theoretical energy-level plots, fitted power-law exponents, instrument settings and measured spectra distinguishable. A near-excitation line attributed to resonant Raman is a source hypothesis, not proof of a separate Raman acquisition. Preserve axis/prose conflicts, explicit power-density units and absent beam geometry or raw values.
- For unspecified solid phases, a formula-unit composition drawing is preferable to inventing a discrete oxide molecule or atomic lattice. Only show valid reference structures when already verified, and label their independence from the source specimens.


## Doped phosphors, comparison recipes and optical scope

Lessons from Yi et al. (2002), DOI 10.1021/cm0115416:

- Preserve a printed reagent formula, mass and mole amount separately when they disagree, including conflicts between the stated feed and nominal final host. Do not silently correct doses or infer exact stock molarities from added solvent volume. Nominal host/dopant notation is not refined occupancy.
- A common preparation can be displayed for each annealing comparison, but individual batch charges, ramps, cooling and atmospheres need their own reported/inherited/missing distinctions. Stage art and chemical reference captions must obey those distinctions. Bulk comparators may use different precursors and atmospheres and need a separate route.
- Keep generic analysis specimens separate when their precise annealing/batch linkage is unreported. TEM particle diameter, Scherrer crystallite size and an intensity-weighted particle-analyzer distribution cannot be merged merely because their central values agree. Retain before/after scale bars and impurity statements; do not invent SAED, atomic coordinates or refined phase purity.
- Read actual concentration markers and fit legends: a stated range does not establish every integer condition, and caption/body/curve discrepancies must remain visible. A laser power rating is not sample irradiance, a power sweep or an absolute yield. Proposed energy transfer, surface/lifetime explanations and application motivations remain author interpretations unless measured.
- When full synthesis records embed upstream stock preparation, distinguish precursor inventory stages needed by the training selector from physical operation stages; verify eligibility against the actual task exporter. Do not promote ambiguous size/outcome contexts to paired labels merely to increase training counts.


## Nanotube composites and separated particle populations

Lessons from Banerjee and Wong (2003), DOI10.1021/ja035980c:

- Map source-defined host acronyms such as MWNT to their verified constituent elements explicitly; do not feed acronyms to an elemental-formula parser. Component-only discovery must name the supported heterostructure and must not claim synthesis of purchased hosts or isolated constituent products.
- Track the retained and removed fractions through every separation. A filter may initially retain both composite and free particles; a later solvent wash separates them. Apparatus and analytical specimen drawings must remove free-particle symbols after purification when depicting the retained composite.
- A tellurium solution in TOP does not by itself establish the starting allotrope, organophosphine chalcogenide speciation, stock concentration or preparation route. Unknown identity needs an explicit composition schematic; reuse the known solvent structure separately.
