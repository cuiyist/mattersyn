## Research landscape discussed

The discussion surveyed literature available through September 2026. The central opportunity is connecting AI-generated candidates to reliable physical predictions, workable synthesis routes, and measured performance. Predicted stability, reproducible synthesis, and useful device performance are distinct achievements.

Six active directions were identified:

1. **General-purpose atomistic models.** Models such as UMA predict energies and forces across materials, molecules, and catalysts. Important unresolved issues include unfamiliar chemistry, interfaces, nonequilibrium configurations, and long-range charge/polarization. Broad chemical coverage does not guarantee application-specific accuracy. References: [UMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23971); [learning electrical response](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-025-01911-z).
2. **Inverse design with multiple constraints.** Generative models propose compositions and structures for target properties. Chemeleon2 uses reinforcement learning to balance novelty, predicted stability, diversity, and properties. Optimization can exploit scoring-model errors, and computational stability does not establish synthesizability. Reference: [Chemeleon2, July 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01262-4).
3. **Synthesis planning and experimental feedback.** DiffSyn generates alternative zeolite recipes and demonstrated an experimental synthesis. A separate perovskite study combined active learning and automated fabrication with measured device results. Remaining problems include kinetics, competing phases, failed-experiment data, and recipe transfer across labs. References: [DiffSyn](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00949-9); [closed-loop perovskite discovery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10482-y).
4. **Models of actual samples and microstructure.** Nominally identical materials can differ because of defects, phase purity, grain structure, and processing history. EXIT incorporates experimental X-ray diffraction into MOF property prediction; PolyMicros models three-dimensional polycrystalline microstructure. Connecting processing, microstructure, and performance remains an opportunity. References: [EXIT](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.6c01049); [PolyMicros](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11055).
5. **More accurate electronic physics.** Neural wavefunctions offer a route beyond simply reproducing approximate density-functional-theory labels. Transferable wavefunctions for solids are promising but remain limited in demonstrated scope and computational affordability. Reference: [transferable neural wavefunctions](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00872-z).
6. **Trustworthy extrapolation and evaluation.** Models need to work on unfamiliar chemistry and express useful uncertainty. UniFFBench reports gaps between computational and experimental performance. A comparison of crystal generation methods found strong simple ion-substitution baselines, emphasizing the need to distinguish stability, structural novelty, and property targeting. References: [UniFFBench](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-01019-4); [generative-model baselines](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5MH00010F).

The assistant's strongest research bets were synthesis models that account for kinetics, uncertainty-guided experiments, and models connecting processing to microstructure and performance.

## Positioning a synthesis-recipe project

**AI for materials synthesis recipes is an active frontier, but plausible recipe generation already has substantial prior art.** A strong contribution should demonstrate an improvement in experimental success, efficiency, reproducibility, or generalization.

Two relevant precedents discussed:

- **DiffSyn**, published in Nature Computational Science in February 2026, models alternative synthesis routes for a desired zeolite and includes experimental validation. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00949-9).
- **Closed-Loop Solid-State Synthesis Planning for Materials Discovery With Large Language Models**, published in Advanced Materials in August 2026, combines literature retrieval, recipe generation, and experimental feedback for inorganic synthesis. Feedback or retrieval plus an LLM alone should not be treated as a new contribution. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.74502).

### Proposed research question

> Can an AI learn from observed impurity phases and processing history to reach a desired material in fewer experiments?

A motivating example is an experiment that produces the intended phase plus unwanted phases. The system would use the measured result to recommend the next precursor combination or processing change, with uncertainty estimates.

The exact novelty needs a targeted literature check after selecting the material family. This is a proposed hypothesis, not a claim of an unstudied method or guaranteed publication.

## Recommended initial scope

## Data strategy

Represent a recipe as **structured, ordered operations linked to the result of that particular experiment**. Useful fields include:

- Target composition and crystal phase.
- Precursors, amounts/ratios, purity, particle size where available, and batch size.
- Mixing, thermal profile, holding times, cooling, atmosphere, and relevant vessel/equipment context.
- Observed products, impurity phases, quantitative phase fractions or other target properties, and measurement uncertainty.
- Source paper or experiment identifier, provenance, and explicitly marked missing information.

### Public starting resource

The December 2025 Scientific Data paper **Text-mined dataset of solid-state syntheses with impurity phases using Large Language Model** provides **80,806 syntheses**, including **18,869 with reported impurity phases**.

- [Dataset paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-06222-y)
- [Downloadable JSON](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.30423274)

Important interpretation: **no impurity reported does not mean verified phase purity**. Literature records can omit failures or relevant conditions and contain extraction errors. Audit the chemistry-specific subset and supplement it with the collaborator's complete records, including unsuccessful, partial, and ambiguous outcomes. Unobserved recipes are not known failures.

## Suggested modeling approach

The core prediction is:

**Target + recipe + laboratory context → distribution of possible experimental outcomes**

Three components were proposed:

1. **Recipe proposer:** retrieve related syntheses and propose several feasible precursor/process combinations.
2. **Outcome predictor:** estimate product phases, phase purity, or target properties, including uncertainty.
3. **Experiment selector:** choose the next experiment using predicted improvement, information gained, and laboratory cost.

A practical first implementation could use:

- An LLM for literature extraction, retrieval assistance, and structured protocol preparation.
- A Gaussian process or tree ensemble as an initial outcome model.
- Bayesian optimization for experiment selection within the lab's executable constraints.

More elaborate generative architectures should be justified by improved measured performance. Multiple recipes can produce the same target, so the system should support alternative candidate routes.

Thermodynamic information may help rank recipes but cannot alone establish reaction pathways or outcomes. Kinetics-aware modeling is a possible source of scientific contribution. The discussion cited a 2026 study combining transport predictions with reaction modeling: [Nature Materials paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-026-02596-5).

## Experimental validation and fair comparison

Agree on the success threshold with the collaborator before evaluating the method. For phase purity, specify the characterization procedure and treatment of measurement uncertainty and amorphous content where relevant.

Proposed comparisons:

- Literature retrieval or an expert-selected starting recipe.
- Conventional Bayesian optimization using the same allowed variables.
- The proposed approach using literature knowledge and impurity feedback.

Methods should receive comparable experimental budgets and initial information. Count and retain every attempt, including partial successes and operational failures. Repeat promising recipes on independent days or batches.

The strongest endpoint is **experiments and elapsed laboratory time required to reach a predefined, verified outcome**. Also assess reproducibility and final material quality. Agreement with a published recipe is an auxiliary metric, since multiple valid recipes can exist.

For retrospective evaluation, keep records from the same paper or closely related campaign together. Evaluate unfamiliar targets or chemistry groups separately and audit near duplicates. Distinguish:

1. Recovering known recipes.
2. Predicting outcomes for unfamiliar recipes.
3. Improving real experiments by following model recommendations.

Only the third directly demonstrates practical synthesis-planning value. Correlations in literature data do not establish that changing a processing condition causes an improvement.
